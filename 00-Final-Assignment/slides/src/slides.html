Why Disentangling months and eqpdays Matters for Intervention Design [13]
  • First-Year Coupling: On a subscriber's first handset (phones == 1), eqpdays ≈ 30 × months. Exactly 0 customers have months ≤ 9 and eqpdays > 300 (max eqpdays for months ≤ 9 is 288 days). Thus, crossing 300 days on a first phone is also crossing into the Month 10–12 contract renewal cliff (29.25% → 64.57%).
  • Independent Device-Age Effect in Mature Cohorts: Holding tenure fixed above 12 months, subscribers with a newer/upgraded handset (≤200d) churn far less than peers on aging handsets (501d+): 39.2% vs. 55.0% in Months 19–24 (-15.7 pp) and 33.2% vs. 57.1% in Months 25+ (-23.8 pp).
So What / Decision Implication: Because first-year contract renewal and handset aging co-move, our Lane 2 intervention pairs a Month 8–9 renewal check with a handset trade-in voucher, verified via an 80/20 holdout pilot [13].

4. PROBLEM FORMULATION & METHODOLOGY

Solving the Case-Control Trap: Re-Basing 49.56% Sample Churn to Realistic 5%–10% Priors [7, 9]

Why Unadjusted Sample Precision Overstates Production ROI by 3.3× [7, 9]
  • The Case-Control Calibration Structure: Company A's dataset has a 49.56% churn rate (49,562 churned vs. 50,438 stayed) [13]. As documented by Neslin et al. (2006) on the Teradata/Duke wireless benchmark [7] and highlighted in the GCI Tutorial PDF, this is a 50/50 case-control sample oversampled from a real operator environment where monthly/quarterly churn is 5% to 10% [3, 7, 8].
  • Ranking Is Invariant, Precision Is Not: Under prior probability shift (where class-conditional feature distributions P(X | Y) remain invariant), a model's ROC-AUC is unchanged, but the fraction of true churners inside the top-k% tier depends directly on the true base rate πr [9].
  • Exact Bayes Prior-Shift Formula (Saerens et al., 2002) [9]: Given sample prior πs = 0.4956, sample top-k% precision Ps, and real production prior πr ∈ {0.05, 0.10}:
Preal(k)  =  [ Ps(k) · (πr / πs) ]  /  [ Ps(k) · (πr / πs) + (1 − Ps(k)) · ((1 − πr) / (1 − πs)) ]

At Top-10% (n = 10,000), sample precision Ps = 81.00% (1.63× lift) maps to Preal = 32.53% (3.25× lift) at πr = 10% and Preal = 18.59% (3.72× lift) at πr = 5% [9].

6. PROPOSED OPERATIONAL SOLUTION

Operational Playbook: A Two-Lane Retention Program Gated by a 90-Day 80/20 Holdout Pilot [7, 8]

Lane 1: High-Value Usage-Slump & Plan-Fit Rescue
  • Target Segment: Top-10% risk score (p̂_i ≥ 0.713) with active revenue (rev_Mean ≥ 30 MU, mou_Mean > 0) exhibiting a 3-month usage drop (mou_ratio_3m < 0.75) or overage friction (ovrrev_Mean > 10 MU).
  • Commercial Offer: Proactive care call + right-sized voice/data bundle, one-time overage buffer credit, and Account Spending Limit (asl_flag = Y) activation.
  • Unit Economics: Expected cost c = 7.50 MU per targeted account; protects V = 328.83 MU (8m margin at 58.72 MU ARPU). Break-even save rate is just s* = 7.01%.
Lane 2: Pre-300-Day Handset Refresh & Month 8–9 Check
  • Target Segment: Active subscribers in the 240–300 day handset window (or Tenure Months 8–9) with rev_Mean ≥ 30 MU (8,815 accounts in 100k; mean ARPU = 66.91 MU, V = 374.70 MU).
  • Commercial Offer: Early handset trade-in / 12-month installment upgrade voucher + quality check for refurbished-device holders (refurb_new == 'R').
  • 90-Day Pilot Math: 80% treatment (7,052 accts) vs. 20% control (1,763 accts) at c = 4.00 MU; closing 25% of the 5.5 pp re-based jump saves 97.0 accounts = +8,125 MU net pilot return.
Guardrail Lane: Automated Digital Triage for Dormant SIMs
  • Target Segment: High-risk accounts with mou_Mean ≤ 10 or rev_Mean < 30 MU (including the 1,615 zero-MOU accounts with 79.81% churn and 23.11 MU ARPU).
  • Why Exclude From Human Calls: At 23.11 MU ARPU, human call + subsidy outreach has poor unit economics because disengagement has already occurred.
  • Automated Action: Zero-marginal-cost automated SMS/push "Win-Back Top-Up Bonus" (c < 0.25 MU) triggered only upon an inbound recharge or usage event.
So What / Decision Implication: Routing high-risk subscribers into cause-specific lanes and keeping a mandatory 20% randomized holdout turns predictive scores into verifiable profit [7, 8].

7. GOVERNANCE, RISKS & EXECUTION ROADMAP

Five Operational Risk Mitigations & a 90-Day Gated Pilot Roadmap Before Full Scale-Up [8, 9]

Key Technical & Commercial Risks Paired with Concrete Mitigations [8, 9, 13]
Risk / Limitation Why It Matters Concrete Mitigation Built Into PoC
1. Observational vs. Causal Uplift High churn risk p̂i does not guarantee a customer will accept an offer (persuadables vs. sure losses). Enforce a 20% randomized holdout control group in Phase 2 to measure true incremental save rate s before scaling budget.
2. Case-Control Prior Drift Model probabilities are calibrated to the 49.56% sample prior rather than live monthly churn (5%–10%). Apply Saerens et al. [9] prior-shift adjustment in batch scoring and monitor monthly Brier/decile calibration.
3. "Sleeping Dog" Trigger Risk Contacting satisfied month-to-month customers about contract renewal can accidentally prompt shopping. Restrict proactive calls to Top-10% risk (p̂_i ≥ 0.713); track weekly treatment-vs-control churn delta by segment.
4. Subsidy Cannibalization Low-risk or low-ARPU subscribers may game handset trade-in vouchers. Enforce rev_Mean ≥ 30 MU floor, mou_Mean > 0, and 12-month installment credit vesting (no upfront cash).
5. Demographic Proxy Bias Neighborhood/credit codes (prizm_social_one, crclscod) could correlate with protected groups. ethnic is excluded (-0.0016 AUC impact); audit offer eligibility parity across regions every 30 days.
90-Day Phased Implementation & Governance Roadmap
  • Phase 1 — Days 1 to 30: Shadow Scoring & Pipeline Integration
    Deploy src/features.py (107 fair features) + final_model.joblib (M5) in monthly shadow mode on billing warehouse; validate data drift, missingness flags (change_mou, hnd_webcap), and CRM call-queue routing.
  • Phase 2 — Days 31 to 60: Launch 80/20 Randomized Holdout Pilot
    Execute Lane 1 (Top-10% usage-slump rescue: 8,000 treatment / 2,000 control) and Lane 2 (eqpdays 240–300d refresh: 7,052 treatment / 1,763 control). Track acceptance rate, call handling time, and 30/60-day survival.
  • Phase 3 — Days 61 to 90: Uplift Readout, Uplift Model & Scale Gate
    Compare 90-day churn between 80% Treatment and 20% Control. Hard Scale Gate: Proceed to full base rollout if verified save rate exceeds break-even (s ≥ 7.01% in Lane 1; net positive MU in Lane 2), then train a two-model uplift ranker on pilot outcomes.
